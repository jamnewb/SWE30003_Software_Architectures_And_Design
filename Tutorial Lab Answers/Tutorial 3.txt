1. Considering the notions of Product and Domain around Lecture 2, Slide 25), is the
receptionist part of the product or part of the domain? Please explain!
The receptionist is part of the domain, this is because those in the domain interact with the product through Product I/O. The receptionist engages in this through interacting with the Product I/O over their computer at their desk at the reception of the building.

2. Given the context of the hotel information system as discussed in Lecture 2, describe the
Check-Out task in the Task & Support style.
Task: Check-out
Purpose: To check the guest out of the hotel
Conditions: The guest has come to reception to check-out
Frequency: Once per hour
Critical: When >30 guests attempt to check-out at once
Sub-tasks: 
1. Guest comes to reception
Problem: Guest may exceed their stay
2. Guest details are verified	Example solution: Verify details with system
Problem: Guest may use an untrustworthy service to provide their ID
3. Guest is removed from guest list	Example solution: Delete guest in system
3a. Variant: Guest may want to extend their stay
4. Guest exits
Problem: Guest may not exit

6. The Check-In task (around Lecture 2, Slide 35) handles two situations:
a) The guest has booked in advance;
b) The guest has not booked in advance, but hopes there is a free room available.
Do the following:
 1) Split the task description into two task descriptions Check-In a Booked Guest and
 Check-In a Non-Booked Guest.


 2) Discuss the difference between the one-task solution and the two-task one. Which
 approach is more suitable?
I believe the two-task approach is more suitable considering should the guest have booked in advance, there is not much work to be completed on the solution, with the receptionist only needing to verify their identity and hand in their key. Considering this, having the booking in advance simply being a variant with the one-task approach makes it harder to tell that certain steps are reserved for the situation in which a booking has not been made in advance. Overall this can make the booking in advance variant seem like more work than it actually is, which does not support the aim of the task description which is to communicate the software solution to the stakeholders effectively.
