Please carefully read the description of the following outline of a software system and within
a group of three to four students, come up with an architectural solution for the given case
study. Justify your solution and discuss the quality implications of the approach taken. 

You can assume that the system is for a medium-sized bookstore with app. 50,000 book titles
on record, on average, about 10,000 books (including duplicates) in stock, app. 20 sales
transactions per hour, and 5 staff members in the store at all times.
Case Study: Bookshop
Goals:
 To reduce the average number of staff members required on-site by at least 15%
within the first 6 months.
 To speed up the average time taken to search for a book in the store by at least 10%
over the first month.
Domain:
 The product shall support adding of books, including their Title, Author, Serial # and
Date of Publication.
 The product shall support selling of books.
 The product shall support returning or refunding of books.
 The product shall support searching for books and offering a guide for finding their
physical location.
 The product shall support categorisation of books by genre (Science Fiction, Horror,
Action, etc.)
Product:
 The product shall have a function for adding and removing books from a database.
This function must ensure there are no duplicate books listed in the database.
 The product shall have a function for altering the on-hand stock count of each book in
the database. When a book is sold, it must decrease the stock count, or when more
stock arrives, it must support incrementing it.
 The product must have a function for recording sales, including which book was sold
and for how much.
 The product must allow users to search the database by either Title, Author, Serial #,
Genre, or a combination of these. This function must return all details on the book,
such as the aforementioned values, the year of publication, the books physical
location in the store, the current stock count for the book, as well as its retail price. 

Answer: Client-server architecture and RESTful web service, with a data-centered approach focused on that which is stored on the server.

This architecture was chosen because of the need to access so many books as a result of the domain being a library. This can be seen in the client-server approach taken where considering all the book data needs to be able to be accessed by the staff members, they can be stored on the server, with all the clients being the computers in the library that will be connected to it. This is also seen in the architecture's characteristic of being data-centered, describing how every client in the design will be accessing a central repository, which is the server, to engage in the library activities desired. As one of the goals was to "speed up ... by at least 10%" this is supported by the lack of a broker pattern. Despite this broker pattern protecting against failures arising as a result of the server being unable to discriminate against requests or getting too many, it is unnecessary as with only an average of 20 transactions per hour, it is unlikely that these dangerous conditions would occur to the server. Furthermore, a lack of a broker pattern speeds up the solution, as it does not need to wait for a response from the broker to access server services. A RESTful web service solution will be deployed as well, allowing clients to access a book database found on the server. This will enable CRUD functionality for the database, allowing the staff to engage in all the "product" functionalities, and will also increase speed over using WSDL as its components are not so hard to modify.
